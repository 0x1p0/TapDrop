# Changelog

What's new in each version, newest first. The notes under a version are what TapDrop shows in its update window, so write them for the people using it. Keep each bullet on one line.

## 2.2.0

### A new look
- **Lumen.** TapDrop is now night, bone white and the cool light of a screen, with no colours of its own: the colour comes from your photos. The new icon is the Notchline, the notch traced in one line of light.
- **Light and dark.** Both apps follow your system, or choose: on the Mac in Settings → General → Appearance, on the phone in Settings → Appearance.
- The page your phone opens from the setup code and the Mac's installer window have the new look too.

### New
- **Forgetting works both ways.** Forget a phone on the Mac and the phone forgets the Mac too, and the other way round, even if one of them is away at the time.
- **Send to Android Phone** in the Mac's right-click Services menu.

### Better
- **The right picture, or none.** With a photo on screen, TapDrop compares only that photo with your gallery, never the app around it, and a match has to clearly beat every other picture. When TapDrop isn't sure, it sends a fresh screenshot.
- **Your whole library.** Every photo and video is ready for tap to send, not only the newest. Settings → Photos & videos for tap shows how many are ready.
- **Videos at any moment.** Pause a video anywhere, even far from its keyframes, and the original arrives.
- **Receipts and documents** saved as photos are told apart by their layout, and a photo shown between a chat viewer's black bars is found in about a second.
- **A self-check on every phone.** If TapDrop ever matches the wrong picture on your phone, it gets stricter there.
- **Taps only when the notch sees them.** A thumb or a pocket no longer lights the phone up: the Mac starts a tap only once its notch sees the light change.
- Queued files and text say plainly whether they're on their way or waiting for your phone to join the same Wi‑Fi.
- Original photos & videos no longer asks for access to your music and audio.

## 2.1.1

### Better
- **Tap to send setup in the right order.** Android only offers "Allow restricted settings" after you've tried TapDrop in Accessibility once, so the checklist now walks you through exactly that: try it, allow it, then switch it on.

## 2.1.0

### Free and open source
- TapDrop is now free and open source under the MIT License. Everything is on GitHub, with a step-by-step install guide.

### New
- **Updates in the app.** TapDrop checks for a new version once a day and shows you what's new. Update with one click on the Mac; on your phone, tap Update and confirm.
- **A proper Mac installer.** Open the DMG and drag TapDrop into Applications.
- **One-time codes you can trust.** Codes are only picked up from messages that are clearly about a code. Amounts, dates, phone numbers and order numbers are left alone.

### Better
- **Pick before sending** gives you more time, and a tap that slides a little or a swipe along a line of text now counts.
- The menu bar panel opens on its own, without bringing other TapDrop windows forward.
- While Settings or Set Up a Phone is open, TapDrop is in the Dock and ⌘-Tab. Close the window and it stays in the menu bar.
- Taps are more consistent: the notch waits for a knock to settle, and the light check adapts to your phone and room.
